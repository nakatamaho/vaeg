HyPER-FRAME+ on the PC-88VA - probes (vaeg M149)

V3 mode, MS-DOS 4.0. Writes A:\HFLOG.TXT. If the photograph and the
log disagree, the log is right. Nothing here touches EC, port 1D0h,
or any memory window; only I/O ports D0, D3-D8, DA, DC, EE.

HFV0            stage 0: does the board answer at all? Read-only.
                PROBE lines show every D0-family port read six ways
                (FF = undriven, D:xx = driven, F:... = floating).
HFV0 /W         adds a bounded write test: the first two writes of
                the JPGVHF viewer's cold initialisation (EE=C0,
                D0=A5), the control readback, and a two-pixel
                write-read-restore at (0,0) and (1,0). Ends with the
                viewer's stop sequence D0=00, EE=00.
HFV1            stage 1: the viewer's initialisation without its
                PC-98 BIOS call, then draws 640x400 (eight colour
                bars on y 0..299, a grey ramp on y 300..399) and
                reads 12 pixels back (RB lines; MISMATCH only means
                the read path differs - look at the monitor anyway).
                Leaves the board displaying; photograph the HF+
                monitor after it ends.
HFV1 /NOINIT    skip the initialisation (draw only).
HFV1 /WORD      select coordinates with word OUTs (D6, D4), as the
                viewer does, instead of bytes in the paper order.
HFV1 /OFF       the viewer's stop sequence D0=00, EE=00.
HFV2            display-enable sweep: after the init and colour bars
                on rows 0-63, writes candidate values to D0 (A1 A3 A5
                A9 B1 E1 E5 21 61 FF) and then to EE (C0 40 80 00 FF)
                with D0=E1, holding each for about 5 seconds. Every
                write is logged with the DOS clock time (the VA clock
                starts at 2000-01-31 00:00 at power-on, so log times
                match a stopwatch). Watch the monitor continuously;
                note the log time of any change. Leaves D0=E1, EE=C0;
                photograph the monitor, then power the machine off.
HFV2 /STOP      only the stop sequence D0=00, EE=00.
HFV3            D3 sweep (D3 was never varied before): phase 1 writes
                D3 = 01 02 03 04 08 10 20 40 80 FF with D0=E1, phase 2
                repeats the D0 sweep with D3=01. 8 s per value.

Run in this order: HFV0, HFV0 /W, then HFV1. If HFV0 shows no
response, HFV1 cannot display anything.

Exit codes: 0 pass, 1 option, 2 machine, 4 log not written;
HFV0: 3 nothing driven, 11h the /W readbacks disagree;
HFV1: 11h a pixel differed.
